Advice & Tips

Here are some key things to think about when decorating your home.

Choosing the Right Color Palette

Step 1: Begin With Yourself

The most natural place to start when choosing a color palette is with yourself. Designer Laurie H. Smith once said, “A woman's home should always be in colors that make her look and feel beautiful.” It's a thought worth holding onto.

The colors that flatter you — the ones you're drawn to in clothing, art, or everyday objects — often translate seamlessly into interiors. These shades feel familiar, effortless, and deeply personal. When a color resonates with you, it brings a quiet sense of comfort into a space.

Step 2: Stay Within the Same Range

Once a core palette emerges, build gently within it. Work with variations of the same hue: lighter and darker tones, softened neutrals, and subtle shifts in intensity.

This approach creates depth without visual noise. Rooms feel layered and cohesive, allowing textures, materials, and light to do the rest of the work.

Step 3: Introduce Thoughtful Contrast

A considered palette benefits from contrast. Opposing tones — whether warm against cool, light against dark, or soft against structured — add balance and interest.

These elements don't need to dominate. A dark wood detail, a black accent, or a cool stone surface can anchor a softer scheme and give the space a sense of intention and quiet confidence.

Mixing Wood Tones

Why Mixing Works

Mixing wood tones brings warmth, depth, and a sense of collected beauty to a space. Rather than matching everything perfectly, variation allows each piece to stand on its own while contributing to a richer whole.

Find a Dominant Tone

Begin with one primary wood tone that anchors the room. This might be your flooring, a dining table, or large cabinetry. Once established, layer in lighter or darker woods as supporting elements.

Create Balance

Pay attention to undertones — warm woods pair best with other warm hues, while cooler woods feel more cohesive together. Repeating tones at different heights helps the mix feel intentional rather than random.

Mixing Textures and Materials

Texture Creates Emotion

Texture is what gives a space feeling. Even a neutral room can feel rich and inviting when layered with contrasting surfaces and materials.

Balance Hard and Soft

Pair smooth materials like glass, metal, or stone with softer elements such as linen, wool, or boucle. This contrast prevents a space from feeling flat or overly rigid.

Repeat for Cohesion

When a texture appears more than once — whether through fabric, upholstery, or decor — it feels deliberate. Repetition is what turns variety into harmony.

How to Use Light to Create Ambiance

Think in Layers

The most inviting spaces rely on layered lighting. Ambient, task, and accent lighting each play a role in shaping mood and function.

Choose Warmth Over Brightness

Soft, warm light instantly makes a room feel more intimate. Avoid relying on a single overhead source — instead, use table lamps, floor lamps, and sconces to create glow.

Use Light to Highlight

Lighting can draw attention to architectural details, artwork, or textured walls. Subtle illumination adds depth and elevates even the simplest spaces.

One Word: Artwork

Art Sets the Tone

Artwork brings emotion, personality, and narrative into a home. It has the power to anchor a room and influence the entire color palette.

Scale Matters

Don't be afraid of large pieces. Oversized artwork can make a space feel intentional and confident rather than sparse.

Choose What Moves You

Art should resonate on a personal level. Whether bold or subtle, the pieces you love will always feel right in your space.
